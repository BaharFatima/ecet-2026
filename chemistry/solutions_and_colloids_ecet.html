Introduction to Solutions & Solubility
▼
What is a Solution?

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances. It has uniform composition throughout.

SoluteThe substance that dissolves (present in smaller amount)
SolventThe substance that dissolves the solute (present in larger amount)
In salt water: Salt = Solute, Water = Solvent. Water is called the "universal solvent".
Solubility

Solubility = Maximum amount of solute that dissolves in 100g of solvent at a given temperature to form a saturated solution.

UnsaturatedCan dissolve more solute
SaturatedMaximum solute dissolved
SupersaturatedContains more solute than saturated (unstable)
For SOLIDS in liquids: Solubility INCREASES with temperature. For GASES in liquids: Solubility DECREASES with temperature (Henry's law).

Atomic Weight, Molecular Weight & Equivalent Weight
▼
Atomic Weight

Atomic Weight = Average mass of one atom of an element compared to 1/12 mass of Carbon-12 atom.

Atomic weight is a relative number (dimensionless). Example: H=1, C=12, O=16, N=14, Na=23, Cl=35.5
Atomic weight is also called Relative Atomic Mass (RAM). It is measured in Atomic Mass Units (amu).
Molecular Weight

Molecular Weight = Sum of atomic weights of all atoms in one molecule.

H₂O= 2(1) + 16 = 18 g/mol
H₂SO₄= 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98 g/mol
NaCl= 23 + 35.5 = 58.5 g/mol
NaOH= 23 + 16 + 1 = 40 g/mol
Equivalent Weight

Equivalent Weight (E) = Molecular Weight ÷ n-factor (valence factor)

E = Molecular Weight / n-factor
Substancen-factorEquiv. Weight
HCl (acid)1 (1 replaceable H⁺)36.5/1 = 36.5
H₂SO₄2 (2 replaceable H⁺)98/2 = 49
H₃PO₄398/3 = 32.67
NaOH (base)1 (1 OH⁻)40/1 = 40
Ca(OH)₂274/2 = 37
NaCl (salt)158.5/1 = 58.5
n-factor for acids = No. of replaceable H⁺ ions. For bases = No. of OH⁻ ions. For salts = total valence of cation × number of cations.

Mole Concept
▼
What is a Mole?

One mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (atoms, molecules, ions). This number is called Avogadro's Number (Nₐ).

1 mole of any substance = Molecular weight in grams = Gram Molecular Mass
1 mole H₂O= 18 g = 6.022×10²³ molecules
1 mole NaCl= 58.5 g = 6.022×10²³ formula units
1 mole O₂= 32 g = 22.4 L at STP
Key Mole Formulas
Number of Moles (n) = Given Mass (g) / Molar Mass (g/mol)
Number of Molecules = n × Nₐ = n × 6.022 × 10²³
Volume at STP (for gases) = n × 22.4 L
STP = Standard Temperature and Pressure (0°C and 1 atm). At STP, 1 mole of any gas = 22.4 L.
📐
Molarity (M)
▼
Definition & Formula

Molarity = Number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution. Unit: mol/L or M.

M = n / V(L) = (W × 1000) / (M.W × VmL)

Where: W = weight of solute (g), M.W = Molar mass, V = Volume of solution in mL

Important Relationships
Dilution FormulaM₁V₁ = M₂V₂
(moles before = moles after)
Mixing FormulaM₁V₁ + M₂V₂ = M_mix × (V₁+V₂)
Molarity CHANGES with temperature (volume changes). Molality does NOT change with temperature.
⚗️
Normality (N)
▼
Definition & Formula

Normality = Number of gram equivalents of solute per litre of solution. Unit: N or eq/L.

N = (W × 1000) / (E × VmL)

Where: E = Equivalent weight, V = Volume in mL

Normality = Molarity × n-factor
Normality in Reactions
At the equivalence point: N₁V₁ = N₂V₂ (Acid-Base titrations)
1N HCl= 1M (n=1)
1N H₂SO₄= 0.5M (n=2)
1N H₃PO₄= 0.333M (n=3)

Mole Concept Problems
▼
How many moles are present in 36g of water (H₂O)?
1
Molar mass of H₂O = 2(1) + 16 = 18 g/mol
2
Moles = Mass / Molar mass = 36 / 18
✓ Moles = 2 mol
Calculate the number of molecules in 44g of CO₂.
1
Molar mass of CO₂ = 12 + 2(16) = 44 g/mol
2
Moles = 44/44 = 1 mol
3
Molecules = 1 × 6.022×10²³
✓ 6.022 × 10²³ molecules
Find the volume occupied by 3.2g of oxygen gas (O₂) at STP.
1
Molar mass of O₂ = 32 g/mol
2
Moles = 3.2/32 = 0.1 mol
3
Volume = 0.1 × 22.4 = 2.24 L
✓ Volume = 2.24 L at STP

Molarity Problems
▼
Calculate molarity of a solution containing 4g NaOH in 200 mL solution.
1
Molar mass of NaOH = 23+16+1 = 40 g/mol
2
M = (W × 1000) / (M.W × VmL) = (4 × 1000) / (40 × 200)
3
M = 4000 / 8000 = 0.5
✓ Molarity = 0.5 M
What volume of 2M HCl is needed to prepare 500 mL of 0.4M HCl?
1
Use: M₁V₁ = M₂V₂
2
2 × V₁ = 0.4 × 500
3
V₁ = 200/2 = 100 mL
✓ 100 mL of 2M HCl needed
Calculate molarity of 9.8g H₂SO₄ dissolved in 250 mL solution.
1
M.W of H₂SO₄ = 2+32+64 = 98 g/mol
2
M = (9.8 × 1000) / (98 × 250) = 9800/24500
✓ Molarity = 0.4 M

Normality Problems
▼
Calculate the normality of 4.9g H₂SO₄ in 500 mL solution.
1
Equivalent weight of H₂SO₄ = 98/2 = 49 g/eq
2
N = (W × 1000) / (E × V) = (4.9 × 1000) / (49 × 500)
3
N = 4900/24500 = 0.2
✓ Normality = 0.2 N
20 mL of 0.5N HCl neutralises how many mL of 0.25N NaOH?
1
Use: N₁V₁ = N₂V₂
2
0.5 × 20 = 0.25 × V₂
3
V₂ = 10/0.25 = 40 mL
✓ 40 mL of NaOH required
A solution of HCl has molarity 2M. Find its normality.
1
HCl has n-factor = 1 (one replaceable H⁺)
2
Normality = Molarity × n-factor = 2 × 1
✓ Normality = 2 N

Introduction to Colloids & Types
▼
What is a Colloid?

A colloid (colloidal system) is a heterogeneous mixture where particles are dispersed in a medium but are too small to be seen and do NOT settle on their own.

TypeParticle SizeExample
True Solution< 1 nmSalt in water
Colloid1 – 1000 nmMilk, Blood, Smoke
Suspension> 1000 nmMuddy water, chalk in water

The dispersed substance = Dispersed Phase. The medium = Dispersion Medium.

Protective Colloids & Gold Number
▼
Protective Colloids

When a lyophilic colloid is added to a lyophobic colloid, it protects it from coagulation. The lyophilic colloid forms a protective layer around lyophobic particles.

Gelatin added to gold sol protects gold sol from precipitation by NaCl. Gelatin is the protective colloid here.
Gold Number

Gold Number = Minimum mass (in mg) of a protective colloid needed to prevent coagulation of 10 mL of standard gold sol when 1 mL of 10% NaCl is added.

Lower Gold Number → Better protecting power of the colloid
Protective ColloidGold Number
Gelatin0.005 – 0.01 (BEST protector)
Haemoglobin0.03 – 0.07
Albumin0.1 – 0.2
Potato starch25
Concept introduced by Zsigmondy. Gold number is a measure of PROTECTING POWER – smaller = better!

Properties of Colloids
▼
1. Tyndall Effect

When a beam of light passes through a colloid, the path of light becomes visible due to scattering by colloidal particles. This is the Tyndall Effect.

True solutions do NOT show Tyndall effect. Colloids DO. This is used to distinguish between them. Sunbeams visible in dusty air = Tyndall effect!
2. Brownian Movement

Colloidal particles show continuous random zig-zag motion due to unequal bombardment by solvent molecules. Named after Robert Brown.

Brownian movement prevents sedimentation of colloidal particles.
3. Electrophoresis

Migration of colloidal particles under the influence of an electric field. Shows colloidal particles are electrically charged.

Positively charged solsFe(OH)₃, Al(OH)₃, TiO₂ → move toward cathode
Negatively charged solsAu, Ag, As₂S₃, starch → move toward anode
4. Coagulation (Flocculation)

Settling/precipitation of colloidal particles is called coagulation. Can be caused by adding electrolytes, heating, or adding oppositely charged sol.

Hardy-Schulze Rule: Higher the valence of added ion, greater the coagulating power. Al³⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Na⁺
5. Adsorption

Colloidal particles have large surface area and can adsorb ions, molecules, or dyes on their surface. This is used in decolourization, purification.

6. Dialysis

Purification of colloids by removing dissolved electrolytes using a semipermeable membrane. Colloid particles cannot pass through. Used in kidney dialysis machines.

📌 Must-Remember Facts
Avogadro's Number = 6.022 × 10²³ (also written 6.023 × 10²³)
Molar volume at STP = 22.4 L/mol (for ANY ideal gas)
Brownian motion is caused by unequal bombardment by solvent molecules — it prevents settling.
Dialysis removes small ions/molecules from colloid using semipermeable membrane.
Electrophoresis = movement of colloid particles in electric field. Electroosmosis = movement of dispersion medium.
Milk is an emulsion (liquid in liquid). Butter is a gel (liquid in solid). Fog is a liquid aerosol.
Delta formation at river mouths occurs because sea salt (electrolyte) coagulates negatively charged clay particles.
